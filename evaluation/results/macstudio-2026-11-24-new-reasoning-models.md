# New reasoning-model pilots: fixed-evidence manual review

## Reviewer compatibility pilots

On the same saved card/customer rewrite draft, LFM2.5 returned empty responses at output budgets of 2,048, 4,096 and 8,192 tokens (the last with a 16,384-token context cap). Magistral returned invalid JSON with a 4,096-token budget and 16,384-token cap. These are unsuccessful review attempts, not accepted or rejected claims; the recorded errors do not prove why the output failed. A separate Qwen 3.5 9B thinking-enabled replay was interrupted after two empty responses at 2,048 tokens, and has no completed-set score. The experimental settings differ from earlier runs. No reviewer was enabled in the app.

## LFM2.5 24B-A2B pilot

Two post-unblinding answers to the same card/customer question were manually checked against their cited passages. Both frozen evidence hashes match the corresponding raw and rewrite arms of the earlier fixed-evidence comparison. The tested tag was `lfm2.5:24b-a2b-q4_0`; generation used thinking enabled, a 4,096-token output limit and temperature 0. Earlier 9B/12B/14B runs used no thinking and a 700-token output limit; GPT-OSS 20B used low reasoning and 2,048 output tokens. The settings therefore do not isolate model choice. This is one sample per arm, on a single question, reviewed with model labels known.

- **Raw arm — unsupported identity and relationship claims; incomplete.** R6 describes FICard as a payment card issued to an account holder and lists its Cardholder attribute; it does not establish the answer's claim that *that* account holder is the particular customer linked to the holding. R1 describes a customer's financialHoldingRole (e.g. owner or beneficiary), not an identity link to a cardholder. R4 states GroupFinancialHolding.Group → Group and mentions familial connections; it does not establish that this cardholder/customer's familial connections are captured through that holding record. The answer omits the permitted Cardholder target types, which are not in the supplied passages, and does not acknowledge the missing evidence. On this same raw arm, 9B and GPT-OSS 20B also conflate a role and a customer link, whereas 14B explicitly notes the relationship is not established; 12B avoids an explicit identity assertion but is incomplete. Generation took **17.3 s**.
- **Rewrite arm — unsupported identity inference; incomplete.** R3 explicitly states FICard.Cardholder → banking Account or Contact, associated with the card. R1 states CustomerFinancialHolding.FinancialHolding → FinancialHolding, described as the unique identifier of a holding connected to a customer. The sentence “That customer is linked” treats the customer associated with the card as the same customer associated with the holding; the two cited passages do not establish record identity. It also omits the Account/Contact targets that R3 actually supplies and does not acknowledge the identity gap. The 9B answer similarly overstates the relationship; the 12B answer mistakes the holding identifier for a customer identifier. The 14B and 20B answers do not directly equate the two records, though they remain incomplete. Generation took **11.4 s**.

These two drafts do not demonstrate a solution to the identity problem or an overall model ranking. Timing includes a single local generation per case and is not a controlled performance comparison. The full prior answer review and pair mapping are in `macstudio-2026-11-24-fixed-evidence-blind-review.md`.

## Magistral 24B pilot

This post-unblinding pilot tested `magistral:24b-small-2506-q4_K_M` once on each arm of the same card/customer question. Both evidence hashes match the corresponding earlier fixed-evidence arms. Thinking was enabled with a 4,096-token output limit, temperature 0 and an explicit 16,384 context cap; the saved runtime reports an actual 8,192-token context. The run finished `completed_with_errors`: **one generated answer and one application extractive fallback**. This small test and its settings do not establish an overall ranking.

- **Raw arm — application fallback; no model draft available to score.** After 21.7 s the application reported that it could not generate an answer with valid source labels and returned excerpts R1–R3. R1 accurately quotes the financialHoldingRole description, but the displayed excerpts do not identify the cardholder or answer how the records relate. This is an unsuccessful generation, not evidence that a model answer was safe or supported. The original model draft is not preserved in this artifact.
- **Rewrite arm — partial; cited associations supported, relationship not resolved.** The generated answer says the card-associated customer can be an Account or Contact [R3] and that CustomerFinancialHolding has a relationship to FinancialHolding [R1]. R3 supports FICard.Cardholder → banking Account/Contact; R1 supports CustomerFinancialHolding.FinancialHolding → FinancialHolding. Unlike LFM2.5's rewrite answer, it does not explicitly claim these customers are the same record. It also does not state the missing identity evidence or explain how, if at all, those specific records relate; it omits CustomerFinancialHolding.Customer. Similar limitations apply to the 14B and GPT-OSS 20B rewrites. Generation took **14.4 s**.

## LFM2.5 24B-A2B: full 12-arm fixed-evidence run

The `answer-lfm24b-12.json` run finished `completed_with_errors`: **10 generated answers and two model errors**. All 12 case/arm evidence hashes match the earlier 9B/12B comparison. It used thinking enabled, temperature 0 and a 4,096-token output limit. Assessments below were made **after unblinding**. The card/customer answers repeat the two pilot drafts above; the pilot and full run are not independent evidence. As with the earlier comparisons, this is one attempt per correlated case/arm, and the different model settings limit comparison.

- **Group holdings, raw — partial; one unsupported example.** R1 describes GroupFinancialHolding as associated with Group and group assets/liabilities; R4 describes customer-owned holdings. R2/R5 describe lines of credit and loans as examples of liabilities, and R3 describes instruments *associated with* holdings, which the answer preserves. But R2/R5 use an overpayment as a balance example; they do not establish “overpayment balances” as a type of FinancialHolding record. The explicit Group/FinancialHolding/Customer relationship fields and fuller holding examples are omitted with no evidence caveat. The 14B answer also avoids 9B/12B's assertion that holdings contain instruments; the 20B answer incorrectly treats a holding-record identifier as a customer link.
- **Group holdings, rewrite — partial; one unsupported example.** Identical to the LFM raw answer and passages; the same supported group/customer descriptions, overpayment-as-holding overreach and missing explicit fields apply. This pair is not two distinct model behaviors.
- **Card/customer, raw — partial; unsupported identity inference.** Same answer as the pilot raw review above: R6 lists Cardholder and describes a card, R1 defines the holding role, and R4 records GroupFinancialHolding.Group → Group. None establishes that the cardholder is the same customer on a holding record or proves that particular customer's familial connection. Account/Contact target types are omitted; the answer does not acknowledge the evidence gap.
- **Card/customer, rewrite — partial; unsupported identity inference.** Same answer as the pilot rewrite review above: R3 supports Cardholder → banking Account or Contact and R1 supports CustomerFinancialHolding.FinancialHolding → FinancialHolding, but the answer's “That customer” treats these as the same record without evidence. It omits Account/Contact targets despite R3, and gives no identity limitation. 14B and GPT-OSS 20B do not explicitly equate the records in their corresponding drafts, although those drafts are also incomplete.
- **Holding details, raw — partial; cited claims supported narrowly.** R3 supports CustomerFinancialHolding.Customer → banking Account/Contact. R2/R4/R6/R1 support the listed balance, loan and investment attributes. It does not claim the unsupported FinancialHolding.Details edge, but neither supplies that requested connection nor acknowledges its absence; it omits the requested substantive definitions of account, loan and investment types. This avoids the invented details-connection claim in 14B and invented Customer targets in GPT-OSS 20B; 12B likewise gives a partial answer without that invented edge.
- **Holding details, rewrite — partial; unsupported details link and savings field.** R3 supports the Customer edge, and the individual FHAccount, FHLoan, FHInvestment, FHSaving and FHLineOfCredit passages support many named fields. The assertion that “the holding contains” the details is not supported by an explicit FinancialHolding.Details edge in R1/R2. In R4, FHSaving has `balanceDefaultDisplayValue`, not the stated `balanceDisplayValue`; the cited passage does not support that exact field. The requested directed connection and type definitions are omitted, without acknowledging the gap. 14B and GPT-OSS 20B also overstate a connection on this arm.
- **Common Account/Contact, raw — model error; no answer to score.** The saved row reports `ModelError: The local model returned an empty answer` after 38.9 s. This is not an extractive fallback or a grounded abstention. The 14B answer on the same evidence incorrectly reverses the Contact → parent edge; 9B/12B give partial drafts and flag the missing Account-side evidence.
- **Common Account/Contact, rewrite — model error; no answer to score.** The saved row likewise reports an empty-answer ModelError; it must be counted as an unsuccessful attempt. The 14B rewrite reverses the Contact → parent edge; 9B/12B provide partial drafts with an evidence limitation.
- **Customer/partner, raw — partial; cited relationship claims supported.** R2 describes CustomerRelationship between a customer and partner where either can be an account or contact; R3/R4 support `customerId` mapping to Account.accountId or Contact.contactId. The answer does not give the requested common Account and Contact definitions or mention partnerId, and does not flag the gap. Earlier model drafts often assert those definitions with mismatched banking or relationship citations; avoiding an invented definition does not make the answer complete.
- **Customer/partner, rewrite — partial; cited relationship claims supported.** R1 describes CustomerRelationship and R3/R4 support both customerId and partnerId mappings to Account/Contact IDs. The common Account and Contact definitions are omitted and their absence is not acknowledged. Other models on this evidence often state definitions with unsupported citations.
- **Card/holding roles, raw — partial; unsupported role location.** R5 lists FICard.Cardholder and describes a card; R4 describes the customer financial holding identifier and other attributes but does **not** define customer roles. The claim that roles are defined in CustomerFinancialHolding is true of the wider schema but unsupported by the cited raw evidence. The answer does not name financialHoldingRole or acknowledge that its defining passage is missing. The other tested models explicitly acknowledge the missing role definition on this arm.
- **Card/holding roles, rewrite — partial; cited claims supported but field omitted.** R6 lists FICard.Cardholder and R1 defines CustomerFinancialHolding.financialHoldingRole. The answer locates the card holder at FICard but does not name its Cardholder attribute, even though R6 supplies it; it correctly names the role field. Unlike the LFM draft, 12B, 14B and GPT-OSS 20B explicitly name both fields; 9B incorrectly says the role field identifies the cardholder.

The full run shows more than the two-answer pilot but remains a small post-unblinding study: two errors, unsupported identity/relationship claims and unacknowledged omissions remain. Exact saved evidence isolates answer generation; it does not demonstrate improved retrieval or a general model ranking.
