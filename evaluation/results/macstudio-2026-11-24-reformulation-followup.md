# Mac Studio reformulation follow-up — 24 November 2026

> The historical raw run artifacts referenced below are not included in this distribution; these results cannot be fully reproduced from this report alone.

The revised prompts reduce some observed unsupported claims but do **not** reliably retrieve all evidence or produce fully supported answers on the four development cases. The revised rewrite for the common Account/Contact case lost both labeled relationship passages that the original rewrite had retrieved. A judge rated the incomplete group-holdings answer 5/5 in both runs. These are diagnostic results, not evidence of a general improvement.

The original development artifact and revised development artifact each record four scored raw/rewrite pairs. Both use the same Nomic embedding model and hybrid index, `qwen3.5:9b-q4_K_M` for rewriting and answering, `gemma4:12b` for judging, and at most six passages with one per source. The revised run uses an anchored query and a stronger evidence-specific answer prompt. Both the query and answer prompts changed; answers may also vary across generations. The revised run records its code hashes and `context_selection: one_per_source`. The original artifact predates that explicit field and used the same selection rule. These paired experiments call the generator directly, **not** the live endpoint's claim review or graph shortcut.

## Claim review and the live path

The review replay sent five manually identified problematic **original** answer arms, with their original citation labels and passages, to the revised `review_answer` using `gemma4:12b`. The human annotations and reference answers were not supplied to the reviewer. Three problematic drafts were accepted; the other two produced invalid retry decisions and review errors. In particular, the reviewer accepted the unsupported cardholder/holding-customer identity claim and the false claim that `financialHoldingRole` identifies the cardholder. The review extracts at most six claims and truncates cited passage text to 1,200 characters, so this replay is a diagnostic sample rather than a general estimate of review accuracy. Acceptance is not proof of support, and an error is not a successful rejection.

The revised live card query used the actual serving path with claim review **disabled**, as in the current configuration. All four selected passages concerned CustomerFinancialHolding; no FICard.Cardholder evidence was selected. Its answer described “a cardholder (or customer)” as linked to a holding through CustomerFinancialHolding.Customer. The cited edge supports a customer link but does not establish that this customer is the cardholder. The prompt change therefore has **not** established that the live answer is safe on this example. The paired-answer script independently calls the generator and a scoring judge; it does not enable the serving claim reviewer.

## Later live retests

These are individual live serving requests with claim review disabled, not reruns of the paired study above. They exercise later evidence-selection and answer guards; the paired results do not measure those guards. All four outputs cite the same pinned Microsoft source revision and report the same index identity as above. The saved live responses do not themselves record an implementation hash.

- In the evidence-selection retest, the generator received four passages, including the explicit FICard.Cardholder→Account/Contact, CustomerFinancialHolding.Customer→Account/Contact, and CustomerFinancialHolding.FinancialHolding→FinancialHolding relationships. Even with those edges present, it inferred that the cardholder and holding customer relate because the fields share target types. The passages do not establish identity of the referenced records.
- In the final card retest, the `verified-graph-response` lists those three extracted references separately and explicitly says shared target types do not establish the same record. Each displayed claim is supported by its corresponding selected relationship passage. This is a useful result for this exact question and does not establish correctness on other relationship questions.
- In the concise overdraft retest, the model answered the definition but added an unrequested statement that the overdraft limit allows withdrawal when the account balance “exceeds zero.” The cited `overdraftLimit` text says the amount can be withdrawn when the account “exceeds balance”; it does not say the account balance exceeds zero, while the entity definition describes withdrawals when the balance reaches zero. The added condition is unsupported and misleading.
- In the final overdraft retest, `verified-catalog-definition` returns the selected FIOverdraft entity's definition directly with its source citation and avoids the added condition. This preserves that catalog wording; it does not independently validate the upstream definition or show that the definition-selection guard chooses the right entity for every paraphrase or ambiguous name.

These two final examples are narrow checks. Broader tests should include other models, ambiguous entity names, multi-part questions and cases where the necessary evidence is absent, while checking that the guard neither invents a match nor hides relevant parts of a question.

The six-case dataset is synthetic/source-derived, published, and not independently reviewed; labels and reference answers are incomplete. Development and test cases share schema sources and families. Inspect the saved full passages and answers before treating an automatic score or a passage-ID match as factual correctness.
