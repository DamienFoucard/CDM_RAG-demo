# Fixed-evidence answer and reviewer comparison — 24 November 2026

> The historical raw run artifacts referenced below are not included in this distribution; these results cannot be fully reproduced from this report alone.

On twelve saved evidence sets, an initial blinded evidence review found more drafts with citation-support problems for Qwen 3.5 9B than for Gemma 4 12B. A later, unblinded review of Qwen 3 14B found both improvements and new errors. GPT-OSS 20B also produced unsupported claims and two fallbacks on the same sets. Most answers from all four models were incomplete. This is a small, non-independent diagnostic sample with one reviewer and does not establish a general model ranking or end-to-end improvement.

## Answer generation with identical evidence

Each generator received the same saved question, original citation labels and source passages for each of six source-derived questions and two retrieval arms (raw or rewritten query), for 48 attempts total. The inputs are from the revised paired runs. The 9B, 12B and 14B models generated all twelve drafts without a recorded fallback or error. The 20B model generated ten answers; two attempts fell back to excerpts after invalid source labels. The run calls `cited_answer` directly: it does not perform retrieval, rewrite the question, invoke the optional claim reviewer, or exercise the live endpoint's deterministic graph and catalog-definition paths. Passages, implementation and model digests are recorded in the local 9B/12B run artifact 14B run artifact, and 20B run artifact; the exact answers and full evidence can be inspected there.

A reviewer assessed the shuffled 9B and 12B drafts against the supplied source text before opening the model key. The same reviewer assessed 14B and then 20B after unblinding the first models, so those reviews were not blind. All twelve evidence hashes match across all runs. The 20B used low reasoning and a 2,048-token output budget; the other generators used no thinking and a 700-token budget, so model and settings both changed. Their full case-by-case assessment and methodology are in the [blind review](macstudio-2026-11-24-fixed-evidence-blind-review.md). The below counts classify a draft as having a support issue where that review identified an unsupported, overstated, generalized, or mismatched-citation claim. They are judgments about the supplied citations, not proofs that the propositions are false everywhere. The two arms per question are correlated; some answers are duplicates. The source-derived cases and provisional reference checklist are not independently reviewed and may omit valid evidence.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qwen 3.5 9B | 12 / 12 | 11 / 12 | 0 / 12 | 8.61 s |
| Gemma 4 12B | 12 / 12 | 5 / 12 | 1 / 12 | 8.39 s |
| Qwen 3 14B (reviewed after unblinding) | 12 / 12 | 8 / 12 | 1 / 12 | 12.16 s |

For 20B the minimum support-issue count includes six generated answers with explicitly identified unsupported or mismatched claims and excludes both fallbacks and ambiguous wording; the mean includes all twelve attempts. Both fallbacks lacked the original model response and cannot be scored as model drafts. The 14B counts classify both group/holding drafts as citation-support issues because the cited passages do not establish their household-specific or holding-to-account/saving assertions. The times are descriptive single-run observations, not controlled throughput measurements. Loading and residency may influence them. Because some selected passages lacked the facts needed for a complete answer, completeness depends partly on the input; the comparison isolates generation only, not evidence availability or retrieval quality. An explicit acknowledgment of missing evidence is preferable to an invented link.

Examples: for the raw card/customer question, the 9B draft incorrectly connected cardholder and holding customer through `financialHoldingRole`; 12B described the available roles without making that identity claim, but still omitted the missing relationship and its limits. For the rewritten card/holding-role question, 9B incorrectly said the holding role identifies the cardholder, while 12B gave the two named fields and supported role examples, with a caveat that its FICard passage merely listed Cardholder. The 14B draft avoided the unsupported cardholder identity claim and explicitly acknowledged the missing relationship on the raw card/customer case. But it reversed the Account–Contact relationship on both common-base drafts and asserted unsupported holding-detail links on both holding-detail drafts. The 20B model invented holding-detail targets, misidentified a customer-holding record ID as a customer link, and on two attempts returned invalid source labels that led to extractive fallbacks. The 9B and 12B models sometimes interpreted financial instruments *associated with* holdings as being *contained in* them, which their cited passage did not establish.

## Optional claim reviewer

Five previously identified problematic **original** answer drafts, including their original contexts and source passages, were replayed with their manual issue annotations withheld. The 9B reviewer accepted all five; the 12B reviewer accepted three and errored on the other two; and the larger Qwen 3 14B reviewer accepted all five without error. The GPT-OSS 20B reviewer rejected three for the manually identified problem and accepted two, with no errors. The 20B replay used low reasoning with a 2,048-token output budget; the earlier models used the existing disabled-thinking, 600-token setting, so model and inference settings both changed. A reviewer error is not a successful rejection. A separate positive-control replay of four manually supported drafts was accepted by the 9B, 12B, 14B and 20B reviewers with no errors. Two positive drafts share the same question and evidence, and the original graph contexts were not saved, so these controls use the saved passages with empty graph contexts. Four cases do not establish a false-rejection rate. Extracted claims and evidence may be truncated by the reviewer. Full records: 9B, 12B, 14B, 20B. The positive-control artifacts record the drafts and decisions for 9B and 12B, 14B, and 20B. The serving reviewer remains disabled; these results do not justify enabling it.

## Larger local model pilot

A single `gpt-oss:20b` reviewer call was started with the same saved card/customer problem and was manually stopped after system memory pressure rose: Ollama showed about 12 GB on GPU with a 4,096-token context, system free memory dropped from approximately 21% to 17%, and swap usage rose by roughly 0.9 GB during the observation. Its saved empty-response error reflects that interruption and is **not** a model-quality result. A retry with the original 600-token output setting completed but returned an empty answer, while a subsequent retry with low reasoning and a 2,048-token budget returned valid decisions for all five drafts, as reported above. During the retry, observed free memory ranged from 19–22% and swap usage rose from 23.3 to 25.0 GiB before stabilizing; these figures describe this shared host and do not establish a safe margin under concurrent workloads. This shared 32 GB machine was not isolated from other services. The model is a larger alternative but is not newer than the Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.5 models already used here. The smaller Qwen 3 14B candidate completed the tests above; this older model family is larger than the 12B model but is not newer than Qwen 3.5 or Gemma 4.
